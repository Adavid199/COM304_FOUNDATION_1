Open-source software is usually free to use, modify, and distribute.

No recurring licensing fees, which lowers total cost of ownership (TCO). Source code is available, allowing organizations to tailor features, integrate systems, and extend capabilities.

Unlike proprietary systems, users are not restricted to vendor-controlled features. Users are not tied to a single company for updates, support, or enhancements.

Easier to migrate, modify, or maintain without relying on proprietary licensing terms or renewal contracts .Open codebase allows independent audits, increasing trust and visibility into vulnerabilities.

Large community involvement often leads to quick security patches and updates. Strong communities provide documentation, forums, plug-ins, and shared solutions.

Innovations happen quickly due to global collaboration. Open-source projects often scale efficiently for enterprise-level workloads (e.g., Linux, Kubernetes).

Systems can be optimized and adapted to specific infrastructure needs. Even if a vendor discontinues involvement, the community or organization can continue development.
