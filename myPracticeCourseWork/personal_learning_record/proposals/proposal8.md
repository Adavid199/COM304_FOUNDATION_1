## Explanation of this proposal
*What do you think this proposal might mean? How might it be implemented?*

The proposal suggests using Artificial Intelligence (AI) to streamline and simplify the council’s operations. Instead of relying solely on manual processes or traditional IT systems, AI would automate routine tasks, analyze data more efficiently, and provide predictive insights to improve decision‑making. Like Proposal 6 (digital‑first citizen engagement) and Proposal 7 (low‑code/no‑code with agile), this proposal is part of the council’s broader modernization push. AI is positioned as a tool to reduce complexity, cut costs, and improve responsiveness in service delivery.Automating repetitive administrative tasks (e.g., scheduling, document processing).

Enhancing customer service through chatbots or virtual assistants.

Using predictive analytics to allocate resources more effectively (e.g., forecasting demand for services).

Detecting anomalies for fraud prevention or cybersecurity. The proposal likely signals a shift in how the council wants residents to access services. In practical terms, it means:

The council website and a mobile app become the main entry points for most public services (e.g., reporting issues, paying bills, finding information, applying for permits). Other channels such as phone, email or in-person visits are still available, but they are no longer the default. They become support options rather than primary ones.

The council intends to provide faster, more consistent, more transparent service delivery through digital tools. The move reflects a desire to reduce costs, modernise services, improve data quality, and make service delivery more efficient.

It encourages residents to use digital channels first, unless they cannot — helping the council focus staff resources on those who genuinely need non-digital support. Modernise the council website to provide clear, consistent access to every service.

Develop or procure a mobile app with key features (notifications, reporting tools, user accounts, payments).

Ensure the platforms are accessible (WCAG-compliant), mobile-friendly, and easy to use. Connect the website/app to internal systems so requests flow directly to relevant teams.

Introduce tracking dashboards so residents can monitor progress.

Improve data sharing across departments to avoid duplication and delays. Launch a communications campaign encouraging residents to use the website/app.

Train staff on new workflows and digital tools.

Position digital as the “fastest and easiest” way to contact the council. Maintain alternative contact channels for those who need them.

Offer digital skills support through libraries, community groups or outreach programmes.

Provide assisted digital options at council offices. Use data from the website/app to identify service gaps or performance issues.

Regularly update the platforms with new features and services.

Adjust internal processes based on user feedback and analytics. The proposal essentially aims to make digital platforms the main gateway for engaging with the council, improving efficiency and convenience while still supporting residents who need non-digital access. Implementation typically involves redesigning services, building strong digital infrastructure, promoting adoption, and maintaining inclusivity.
